How a bottle works

Four steps, from recording your voice to the moment it reaches them.

1

Record a message

A voice note or a short letter, for someone specific, about something specific.

2

Attach Bitcoin

Pay in with M-Pesa. Any amount — this isn't about how much, it's about when.

3

Choose the day

A birthday, a graduation, an anniversary. The bottle stays sealed until then — provably, by Bitcoin itself.

4

It opens on time

They're notified, the note plays, and the Bitcoin becomes theirs to keep or cash out to M-Pesa.

Nothing to learn

The sender pays in shillings. The recipient cashes out to M-Pesa. Bitcoin does the locking quietly, in the background.

Sealed, not promised

The unlock date isn't a policy someone could change. It's built into the transaction itself.

Small amounts welcome

This isn't an investment product. It's a way to say something, backed by something — starting from a few hundred shillings.

Who it's for

Built first for Kenyans abroad who already send money home — and want to send more than money.

The milestone you'll miss

You know you won't be home for their 18th birthday. Record something now, so a part of you is there anyway.

The gift that waits

School fees sent home too early get spent on something else. A bottle only opens when it's actually needed.

A harambee that arrives later

Friends and family can each fund the same bottle — a quiet, modern harambee that lands on exactly the right day.
